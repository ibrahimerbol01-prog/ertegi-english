TITLE: Maqta Qyz and the Cat
LEVEL: C1
TARGETS: cat, tail, milk, egg, tree, grain, mouse, leaves, raisin, shopkeeper, quarrel, millet
---
[1] Maqta Qyz, whose name means "cotton girl", was tidying her home when something small and dark caught her eye. It was a single raisin, sweet and wrinkled, as precious to a child as a coin. Her first thought was a generous one. A treat tastes better when it is shared, so she called the cat to come and have its part. The cat, however, did not come. With the logic of someone whose kindness has been ignored, she ate the raisin herself and declared that the cat could keep away from her for good.
[2] When the cat at last strolled in and asked, quite innocently, why it had been called, Maqta Qyz refused to explain. Pride had replaced generosity, and the conversation went downhill fast. The cat, offended by her silence, threatened to tip over her qatyq, a sour milk drink. The girl answered threat with threat: if the cat dared, she would cut off its tail. Neither was willing to back down, and so both promises were kept. The milk ran across the floor, and the cat's tail was cut off. In a matter of moments a quarrel over one raisin had cost them both something they valued.
[3] It did not take long for the cat to regret its temper. A cat without a tail is a sorry sight, and it begged for the tail to be returned. Maqta Qyz, still smarting, agreed on a single condition: what had been spilled had to be replaced. So began the cat's errand, which turned out to be far longer than it had imagined. The cow would gladly give milk, she said, if only she were not so hungry; she needed leaves. The tree would happily part with its leaves, but it was parched and wanted water. A group of girls had water to spare, yet they would trade it only for chewing gum. The shopkeeper who sold the gum named his price, an egg, and the hen who might lay that egg asked, reasonably enough, for grain.
[4] Somewhere along that road, with every door opening only onto another request, the cat must have asked itself whether a tail was really worth so much trouble. Each favour depended on another, like beads on a string that would not hold until the last one was in place. Should it give up and learn to live with its loss, or should it keep going, step by tired step, until the whole chain was complete?
[5] The cat chose to keep going, and its patience was rewarded in the most unexpected place. A mouse, busy digging a home for itself, had a plate of millet beside its burrow. It did not bargain or set conditions; it simply gave the cat a handful of grain. Of all the creatures on the road, the little mouse was the only one who asked for nothing in return, and that single act of kindness set everything else in motion.
[6] From then on the chain unravelled in reverse, link by link. The hen pecked the millet and laid an egg, and the egg bought the gum from the shopkeeper. The gum won the girls' water, and the water revived the tree, which let its leaves fall. The leaves fed the cow, and the cow gave milk at last. When the cat set the sour milk in front of Maqta Qyz, she returned its tail, and the quarrel that had begun over one raisin was finally laid to rest. Neither of them, one imagines, forgot how much a moment of stubbornness had cost.
[7] The tale leaves its lesson unspoken, but it is hard to miss. What greed and pride break in an instant, only patience, honest effort and a little kindness from others can put right.
